## 1. Purpose and editorial conclusion

This post should answer a narrower question than “which one is correct?”

MBTI and Saju can both become *languages people use to reflect on themselves*,
but they do not establish a complete, fixed, or scientifically interchangeable
account of a person. They arise from different premises, ask different kinds of
questions, and should not be used to make consequential decisions about a
person.

The post should leave the reader with a practical standard: use a framework as
an occasion for reflection and conversation, then test any insight against lived
experience, context, and the person’s own choices. Do not use it to confine
yourself or someone else to a label.

## 3. Interpretation and cultural framing

The following are editorial interpretations, not empirical claims to present as
facts:

- Saju is a traditional interpretive system that reads a birth-time structure;
  it is not a psychological assessment equivalent to MBTI.
- Both frameworks can feel meaningful because they give people vocabulary for
  noticing patterns, questions, values, and relationships.
- Feeling understood by a description can be a starting point for reflection;
  it is not proof that the description completely or uniquely captures someone.
- A person changes across situations, relationships, roles, and time. A label
  may be useful only while it remains open to revision and lived evidence.

Avoid presenting Saju as scientifically validated, using Saju to diagnose
personality or predict an individual’s future, or treating MBTI and Saju as if
they measured the same construct.

## 4. Recommended article structure

1. **Opening question** — “MBTI는 INFP, 사주는 어떤 일주… 두 설명이 모두
   나 같다고 느껴질 때가 있다.” Acknowledge the familiar experience without
   confirming that either framework has captured an essence.
2. **They begin differently** — Briefly contrast a self-report preference
   framework with a traditional birth-data interpretation framework. State that
   the difference means a direct winner/loser comparison is misplaced.
3. **Why both can feel persuasive** — They supply words and prompts for
   reflection. Mention the Barnum effect carefully: general descriptions can
   also feel uniquely personal, so resonance merits curiosity rather than blind
   acceptance.
4. **What they cannot decide** — Neither should choose a person’s job, partner,
   medical care, legal choice, or worth. MBTI is explicitly not a hiring or
   diagnostic tool; Destiny also treats Saju as reflection/cultural content, not
   professional advice.
5. **Closing practice** — Ask readers to keep what helps them notice a pattern,
   question what boxes them in, and let present choices remain theirs.

## 5. Tone and safety guardrails

- Use “설명하는 언어”, “참고”, “질문”, and “돌아보기”; avoid “본질”,
  “정답”, “운명적으로”, “반드시”, or “당신은 원래”.
- Do not ridicule MBTI users or people interested in Saju.
- Do not place a reader in a type or Saju category from a comment or anecdote.
- Do not provide personal readings or advice about health, law, money, safety,
  employment, or relationship decisions.
- Do not use a person’s birth data, test result, or private story as an example
  without explicit permission. Prefer a synthetic example or none.
